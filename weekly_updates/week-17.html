Part 2 — Paper Presentation

Paper: Toward Data-Driven Surrogates of the Solar Wind with Spherical Fourier Neural Operator

Motivation

  • The paper focuses on simulating and investigating variations in the solar wind.
  • Traditional 3D magnetohydrodynamic models are accurate, but computationally expensive.
  • MHD models solve three-dimensional physical equations, which makes repeated simulation costly.
  • Machine-learning surrogates are useful because they approximate these simulations without solving the full equations every time.
  • The goal is to support faster forecasting and uncertainty quantification.

Background

Operators

A standard function maps a vector to another vector. An operator maps a function to another function. This is useful for learning physical fields such as solar wind velocity.

Fourier Neural Operator

FNO learns mappings in Fourier space. Convolution-like operations become multiplication in the spectral domain, making the model efficient for PDE-like problems.

Spherical Fourier Neural Operator

FNO is designed for Euclidean grids. SFNO extends this idea to spherical domains using the Spherical Harmonic Transform.

SFNO Transform Flow

Spherical Harmonic Transform → multiply spherical harmonic modes → inverse Spherical Harmonic Transform.

Approach

The authors propose an SFNO-based machine-learning surrogate for steady-state solar wind modeling. The model is trained to predict the radial solar wind velocity field:

$$v_r(\phi, \theta, r)$$

$$Input: v_r(\phi, \theta, r_0)$$ $$Output: v_r(\phi, \theta, r_1) ... v_r(\phi, \theta, r_{139})$$

Here, \(\phi\) and \(\theta\) represent angular position on the sphere, and \(r\) represents radial distance from the Sun. The ground truth comes from MAS MHD simulations.

The SFNO model is compared with HUX-f, a reduced-physics numerical surrogate baseline that propagates solar wind velocity from 30 R⊙ to 1 AU, approximately the Sun–Earth distance.

Data

Item Description
Samples 1,069 samples from 616 Carrington rotations.
Time Span 1975 to 2025.
Grid Shape (140, 111, 128), corresponding to radius, latitude, and longitude.
Input The first radial slice, r0 = 30 R⊙.
Target The remaining 139 radial layers, from near the Sun to 1 AU.

Architecture

The architecture follows this high-level pipeline:

Input radial slice → Encoder MLP → SFNO blocks → Decoder MLP → Predicted velocity field
  • The model uses positional embeddings and skip connections for stable prediction.
  • During SHT \(\alpha_{lm}\) is computed.
  • It uses 110 latitude modes based on Gauss–Legendre sampling.
  • It uses 64 longitude modes based on the Shannon–Nyquist sampling limit.
  • No tensor factorization is applied, preserving maximum learning capacity.

Evaluation

The paper evaluates SFNO using several complementary metrics:

MSE Edge-specific MSE Wasserstein Distance MS-SSIM PSNR ACC

Results

  • The best-performing model is the 8-layer, 256-channel SFNO.
  • It outperforms HUX-f in overall MSE and edge-specific MSE.
  • SFNO also shows strong performance in high-gradient solar wind regions.
  • The model demonstrates resolution invariance: it can produce consistent radial velocity structures without retraining when the input is upsampled.
Important interpretation: SFNO performs better across the reported quantitative metrics, but HUX-f can sometimes produce smoother-looking large-scale physical structures. This suggests that solar-wind-specific evaluation metrics may be needed.

Conclusion

The paper demonstrates that SFNO can serve as a fast and accurate data-driven surrogate for solar wind modeling. It can approximate high-fidelity MHD simulation outputs while being more efficient than repeatedly solving the full physical equations.

Future Work

  • Design better physics-aware loss functions.
  • Create solar-wind-specific skill scores.
  • Incorporate additional physical variables, such as plasma mass density \(\rho\).
